Computational Chunking and Cross-Linguistic Audio Analysis in Digital Humanities

While parallelism between music and natural language exists at the surface structure level in phrasing, metric patterns, and group boundaries, mapping deeper cross-domain correlations remains a complex challenge. This research investigates how prosodic and linguistic features such as rhythm, stress, and intonational pitch contours interact with melodic chunking and musical structure during cross-linguistic adaptation. The core objective is to evaluate how speakers of diverse language families adapt their native idioms to fit a fixed vocal melody while balancing two competing constraints: maintaining semantic fidelity and storytelling content, and preserving musical integrity across pitch contours, metrical accentuation, and diastematic intervals.

By analyzing adaptations across distinct rhythm-typology groups, such as syllable-timed languages like Italian and Spanish, stress-timed languages like German, and mora or tone-based languages like Chinese and Japanese, this framework captures the cross-cultural variations, prosodic shifts, and morphological adjustments that emerge when language is constrained by musical time. The experimental pipeline begins with textual translation and natural adaptation into target languages without rigid structural constraints, followed by audio capture of vocal performances across native language cohorts. The audio signal processing pipeline then extracts F0 pitch tracking to measure fundamental frequency deviations, generates prosograms for visual prosodic analysis, and evaluates structural chunking alignment between linguistic units and musical boundaries.

The technical framework integrates the formal principles of Fred Lerdahl and Ray Jackendoff's Generative Theory of Tonal Music with an audio processing stack built in Python using Librosa for feature extraction, NumPy and SciPy for matrix manipulation and scientific signal analysis, and Matplotlib for visual data representation. The resulting analysis of prosodic deviations, involuntary errors, and syntactic adjustments reveals how native language traits bend under musical constraints. Beyond theoretical musicology, this methodology provides computational models and actionable insights for multilingual media adaptation, dubbing, and cross-linguistic prosodic analysis.
